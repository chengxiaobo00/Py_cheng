# Definition  
  
Reading Lists are lists of books, articles or other media that an individual has chosen to add to their personal reading collection based on interest and availability.

A **reading list** includes a formal recommendation system for books which the reader has selected at least before the beginning by making notes of book titles, authors, and publication information. A "bookmarking" system is also common; readers might even write down an abbreviated summary or brief review of each item on their reading list (often referred to as "reading notes").

### How it Works  
Bookmarked items are usually added and maintained via digital tools such as bibliographic databases which allow tracking individual reading lists, including those without a formal system in place. Some readers have also adopted traditional methods that can be time-consuming but provide for greater personal input and interaction.

A classic example is the use of paper note cards where specific information could easily be noted down by hand while researching or previewing titles; many people continue to use such tools with digital counterparts, simply swapping out writing mediums. An alternative method used widely today involves adding a URL into a favorite section on a web browser's bookmarks bar for easy reference to future browsing.

### Variants  
**Bookmarked Reading List**: This method of tracking reading list includes the notes taken by a reader that might be later updated with additional interest information.

**Collaborative Reading List**: Involves an ongoing collective effort among individuals, typically found within group projects or online communities. Each participant contributes books in their collection to share or promote ideas or discussions.
