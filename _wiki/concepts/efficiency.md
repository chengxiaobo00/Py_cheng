**Concept ID**: efficiency

### Definition

Efficiency is the degree or extent to which something is done with minimal effort and without waste. It measures how effectively resources are utilized, resulting in a desired outcome or achieving goals efficiently.

### How it Works - Technical Explanation

Efficiency is quantified using metrics such as energy efficiency (in relation to mechanical systems like machinery), electrical efficiency (measuring power usage versus potential output), information processing efficiency (for computers and data analysis), material use efficiency (in construction projects regarding materials), time management efficiency (a project completion over the planned deadline).

Efficiency can be increased through various means including technological advancements, better design, automation, optimization of processes, reengineering procedures, prioritization of tasks according to importance or urgency, skill development for workforce and consumers alike. The primary goal is always an improvement in the utilization rate without unnecessary consumption or waste.

### Variants

While efficiency itself deals with optimal performance and reduction of inefficiency, there are several related concepts:

- **Productivity**: Refers specifically to how much output is generated by a unit of input (often time). Productivity can be higher than overall efficiency because it considers the effectiveness over specific activities.
  
- **Resource Utilization**: This concept focuses on optimizing allocation and usage of resources such as people, equipment, materials, etc. It includes efficiency in production or service provision.

- **Cycle Efficiency**: A type of process optimization that examines the stages within a continuous workflow (e.g., manufacturing) rather than at a whole system level.

- **Non-Economic Efficiency**: This principle applies where an economic action is judged by its outcomes for all parties affected, including environmental factors, ethical implications, and social justice. Non-economic efficiency does not seek to maximize output; it emphasizes making decisions in ways that align with holistic well-being without seeking absolute increase.
