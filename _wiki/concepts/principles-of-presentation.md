# Concept ID: principles-of-presentation
### Definition
Principles of presentation refer to a set of guidelines or techniques that can be used in order to achieve effective communication across various mediums, including slides and verbal presentations.

### How it works

Presentation principles include best practices such as clear messaging, engaging visuals, appropriate structure, repetition and practice. Each element should work together to enhance the overall effectiveness and retention rate of a presentation.
  
- **Clear Messaging:** Use language that is concise and accessible, focusing on key points without diluting main ideas or confusing listeners.
  
- **Engaging Visuals:** Utilize graphics, charts, and other visuals that are designed not just for aesthetic purpose but for their ability to convey important information clearly.

- **Appropriate Structure:** Maintain an outline or framework that guides the flow of content. Consistency in organization is crucial.
  
- **Repetition and Practice:** Enhance memory retention by repeating key concepts throughout your presentation and through rehearsal.
  
### Variants

There are numerous variants on specific principles, commonly found in books, online courses, and academic papers about effective communication methods.

1) **Slide-Glossary**: This involves using concise lists of terms to define them as you display text or figures on slides. It helps keep the information fresh during a slide talk by briefly reviewing content with your listeners.
   
2) **Presentation-Scripts**: Some presentations benefit from having a set script that is memorized and repeated, providing structure similar to that of a monologue.

## The Importance of Effective Presentation

In today's world where information is often received through digital interfaces, effective presentations play a significant role in conveying complex ideas succinctly and attractively. By mastering these principles and implementing them correctly, presenters can avoid numerous communication problems such as losing focus due to a poor plan or presentation cluttered with irrelevant information.

Furthermore, the audience usually remembers two-thirds of what they see compared to only one-third of what they hear. Slides therefore become an extremely valuable tool for enhancing retention rates if used in conjunction with effective oral explanation and compelling visuals.

By following these basic principles of good communication, every presenter can improve the clarity and impact of their presentations while also ensuring that their message resonates well within a limited time frame.
