### Definition

Financial stability, also known as macroeconomic stability or fiscal solvency, refers to a country's ability to manage its money supply and balance of expenditures. It is an important concept in finance and economics, as it helps assess whether a nation is able to avoid disruptions through economic crises.

### How it Works

In simple terms, financial stability can be achieved when:

1. The national debt is manageable.
2. Money flow within the economy remains smooth without major interruptions or crashes (inflation).
3. Excessive money creation does not lead to asset bubbles, hyperinflation, and eventual collapse of confidence in a country's currency.

This condition ensures that interest rates are stable and can be set at reasonable levels. Financial stability leads to predictable savings, investments, and economic growth over time.

### Variants

- Money supply: A larger money supply than needed.
- Currency crisis: The failure of a country’s monetary policy leading to high inflation or currency depreciation.
- Debt overhang: Debt that is not being paid off on time, accumulating past due taxes, and disrupting the ability to pay down current debts.
